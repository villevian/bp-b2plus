Cheat sheet: how to talk about market researchШпаргалка: як говорити про маркетингові дослідження

The main idea of the unit: business decisions should be based on data about people, not on the boss’s opinion. In tech the same thing is called user research, product discovery or analytics.Головна думка юніту: бізнес-рішення мають спиратися на дані про людей, а не на думку керівника. В IT це те саме, тільки називається user research, product discovery, analytics.

Answers and model answersВідповіді та зразки

Video A — Gustaf Alströmer

Part 1 · 00:42–03:23

  1. When do great founders start talking to users? — Before they even have a product — and they keep learning from users throughout the life of the company.
  2. Why do users “keep you honest”? — They are the only stakeholders who actually pay you, so they will tell you the truth.
  3. Who are the only stakeholders who pay? — Users and customers.

Part 2 · 05:58–09:42

  1. Best format? — A video or phone call (or in person).
  2. Why not pitch early? — You get polite reactions to your idea instead of real information about their problems.
  3. Your role? — To listen, not to talk — and to ask open-ended follow-up questions.

Part 3 · 09:42–17:04

  1. Questions to avoid? — Yes/no questions, “Would you use this?”, “Which features do you want?”
  2. The one big danger? — Focusing on features instead of problems.
  3. Good problems, bad solutions? — Users know their problems well, but their ideas for solutions are often bad. The solution is your job.
  4. Is the problem valuable? — People already pay for solutions; free tools like spreadsheets aren’t strong competitors; the audience is easy to sell to.
  5. Prototype? — Watch without giving instructions; ask them to think aloud.
  6. Keep interviewees involved? — A shared group chat, share progress, make them feel special.

Video B — Eric Migicovsky

Part 1 · 00:27–02:43

  1. What do the best founders do? — They maintain a direct connection to their users throughout the life of the company.
  2. The YC motto? — Write code and talk to users.

Part 2 · 02:43–05:56

  1. Three mistakes? — Talking about your idea instead of their life; hypotheticals instead of specifics; talking instead of listening.
  2. Why are hypotheticals dangerous? — People are too optimistic about the future; answers don’t show what they will really do.

Part 3 · 05:56–14:16

  1. Five questions? — What’s the hardest part about…? When was the last time you encountered…? Why was it hard? What have you done to solve it? What don’t you love about the solutions you’ve tried?
  2. Which question shows the problem is real? — “What have you done to solve it?” — if nothing, the problem may not be painful enough.
  3. Why “the last time”, not “usually”? — A real event gives facts; “usually” gives general, often inaccurate answers.

★ Which method?

  1. Cancel after a month → in-depth interviews (qualitative, primary)
  2. Which button gets more clicks → A/B test (quantitative)

Answers and model answersВідповіді та зразки

10A — model (Duolingo vs Babbel)

Duolingo attracts customers with a free app and games; its target audience is people who want to learn a little every day. Its promise is that learning is fun, and customer satisfaction is high because of streaks. It’s memorable because of the green owl — that’s real impact on social media. Babbel is a formidable competitor for serious learners: it’s viable as a paid product because its ideal customer has a clear pain point — they need a language for work.

10B — better questions

  1. Tell me about the last meeting you had to summarise. How did you do it?
  2. What’s the hardest part about … at the moment?
  3. What have you tried so far to solve this? What don’t you love about it?
  4. What are you paying for tools like this right now? (the original question tag biases the answer)

Good survey (AI meeting summary in Slack): 1 How many meetings do you have in a typical week? (multiple-choice, quant.) · 2 Do you write meeting notes yourself? (yes/no, quant.) · 3 How satisfied are you with your current notes, 1–10? (rating, quant.) · 4 Tell us about the last time a decision from a meeting got lost. (open-ended, qual.) · 5 What tools do you use for notes now? (multiple-choice) · 6 What don’t you love about them? (open-ended, qual.)

10D — model script

Our target audience is product managers in remote teams. Over half of the respondents admitted that they lose decisions after meetings, and two of them raised concerns that AI notes are not accurate. The burning problem is not taking notes — it’s finding decisions later, so it warrants solving. As a next step, we’ll roll out an MVP in Slack and run an A/B test with 50 users to get hard data.

★ Stakeholder challenges — model replies

  • Hard data or compliments? — This isn’t a compliment — it’s what they actually did: three of five had lost a decision last week.
  • Five people isn’t a sample size. — It’s a small sample, but the pattern was clear. Next we’ll run a survey with 200 respondents.
  • Features instead of problems? — No, we started from the pain point; the feature comes second.
  • Is it a burning problem? — Yes — they already pay for two tools and still lose decisions.
  • Can’t AI do this research? — AI can speed up synthesis, but it can’t replace talking to real users.
  • Formidable competitor? — Moving users off their tool is notoriously difficult, so we’ll integrate with it, not replace it.
